Payment-Date Prediction

A two-stage system that flags, before the due date, which account statements are likely to be paid late and predicts when each one will be paid, so the Collections team can act preventively instead of chasing missed payments.

01Business context

The company issues corporate credit cards for businesses. Clients spend on their cards during the month and then receive a billing statement with a due date.

About 80% of statements each month are paid on time. The Collections team then works to recover the remaining debt, with a goal of reaching 97% of the month's total debt. The earlier they know which clients are likely to pay late, the more room they have to act before the due date passes.

02The problem

Whether a client pays on time is only known once the due date has passed, which is too late to prevent anything. Collections needed two answers for every new statement, before the due date:

  • Whether it will be paid late, so the right clients get preventive actions
  • When it will be paid, so each client has an expected payment date
I split the work into two stages: a classifier that answers whether a statement will be paid late, and a regressor that answers when it will be paid. The classifier's probability of late payment is one of the regressor's inputs.

04Data

1M+ account statements from the last two years, with:

  • Amount owed, amount paid and payment date, which give each client's payment history
  • Company characteristics: size, country, income and more

From the same statements I derived the two targets: late vs on time (payment after or on/before the due date) for the classifier, and days to pay for the regressor.

05Solution architecture

Statements are prepared and turned into features once. The classifier scores each statement's probability of late payment and assigns a risk segment; the regressor uses that probability, among other features, to predict the payment date. Both run in production on Databricks every time statements are issued.

06Stage 1 · Late-payment classifier

A LightGBM binary classifier predicts whether a statement will be paid after its due date (late) or on or before it (on time). Since about 80% of statements are paid on time, late payers are the minority class. I used a stratified train/test split, so both sets keep the same share of late payers, and stratified cross-validation. Hyperparameters were tuned with Optuna.

I compared three ways of handling the imbalance:

ApproachHow it worksOutcome
SMOTEOversamples the minority class with synthetic late payersTested
Random undersamplingDrops on-time statements to balance the classesTested
LightGBM scale_pos_weightGives late payers more weight during training, with no resamplingChosen: best performer
The model was optimized for recall: missing a client who will pay late costs more than an extra preventive reminder to one who would have paid on time. Recall is not a single number here, because it depends on the probability threshold, which is why the thresholds were chosen together with Collections.

07Risk segments with Collections

Rather than a single cut-off, I worked with the Collections team over several meetings, using precision and recall vs threshold charts to show what each threshold would catch and how many clients it would flag. Together we defined three segments by the predicted probability of late payment:

Risk segmentPredicted probability of late payment
Segment 150–65%
Segment 265–80%
Segment 380–100%

Each segment gets different preventive actions before the due date, drawn from reminders, WhatsApp messages and friendly calls about the due date.

08Stage 2 · Payment-date regressor

A LightGBM regressor predicts days to pay, which gives the predicted payment date. It is trained on all account statements, not only those flagged by the classifier, and takes the stage-1 probability of late payment as an input alongside the statement and client features.

  1. EDADistributions and payment behavior.
  2. PreprocessingMissing values and data types.
  3. FeaturesLag variables, interactions, numeric encoding.
  4. Model selectionLinear regression, XGBoost and LightGBM compared on MAE; LightGBM won.
  5. TuningBayesian optimization with Optuna.
  6. ValidationStratified cross-validation and testing before deployment.
MAE was the selection metric because it reads directly in days: an MAE of 1.86 means the predicted payment date is off by under two days on average.

09Production

Both models run in production on Databricks, with unit tests to check that everything works correctly. Predictions run every time statements are issued: there are 14 billing dates per month, and new statements are scored on each of them. 10Results

Trained on 1M+ account statements, the payment-date regressor predicts the payment date with a mean absolute error of 1.86 days, and the classifier sorts every new statement into a risk segment on each billing date.

11Business impact

  • Collections sees late-payment risk before the due date for every new statement, instead of reacting to a missed payment
  • Preventive actions are matched to each risk segment rather than spread across the whole portfolio
  • Each client has a predicted payment date, so the team can prioritize, not only flag
  • Scores refresh automatically on every billing date and land in the Metabase dashboard Collections already uses
  • Acting earlier on likely late payers supports the team's goal of recovering 97% of each month's debt
