01Business context

The company issues corporate credit cards for businesses. Clients spend on their cards during the month and then receive a billing statement, which they pay on a date of their own choosing, sometimes on time, sometimes late and sometimes not at all.

That payment date matters to the risk and collections teams: it shapes cash expectations and credit risk, and the earlier they know a client is likely to pay late, the more options they have to act.

02The problem

Whether a client will pay on time is only known once the due date has passed, which is too late for the risk and collections teams to prevent anything.

The goal was to predict, for each client, how many days it will take to pay its statement, so clients predicted to pay late or not pay at all can be identified up front and handled with preventive actions.

I framed it as a regression on days-to-pay rather than a late / on-time label, so the output says how late, not only whether.

As the Data Scientist on the project, I owned the work from the raw statements to the deployed model.

04Data

Every account statement from the last two years, with:

  • Amount owed, amount paid and payment date, which give the target (days to pay) and each client's payment history
  • Company characteristics: size, country, income and more

05Solution architecture

The solution runs end to end on Databricks: statements are prepared and turned into features, several models compete on error, the best one is tuned and validated, and the deployed model produces a predicted days-to-pay for each client.

06Tools & methods

  1. 01
    Supervised regression on days-to-payPredicts a number of days per client, not a yes / no flag, so teams can rank clients by expected delay.
  2. 02
    Lag features and interactionsPast payment behavior and combinations of variables give the model each client's history and context.
  3. 03
    Model comparison by MAESeveral ML models in scikit-learn were trained and evaluated; the lowest mean absolute error decided, since the error reads directly in days.
  4. 04
    Bayesian hyperparameter optimizationOptuna searches the settings of the selected model more efficiently than a manual or exhaustive search.
  5. 05
    Databricks, Python, SQL and PandasData access and preparation in SQL and Pandas, modeling in Python, and deployment on Databricks.

07Results

The final model predicts the payment date with a mean absolute error of 1.86 days.

08Business impact

  • The risk and collections teams can identify clients predicted to pay late or not pay at all before it happens, instead of reacting to a missed payment
  • Preventive actions can be targeted at those clients rather than spread across the whole portfolio
  • Each client gets an expected payment timing in days, so the output can be used to prioritize, not only to flag
  • The model is deployed on Databricks, so predictions are available to the teams that need them, with an average error of 1.86 days
